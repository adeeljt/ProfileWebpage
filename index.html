I am an Assistant Professor in the Faculty of Information Technology at the University of Central Punjab (UCP), Lahore, where I also serve as Director Graduate Programs. My research is in aerial and mobile robotics, and specifically in using cameras to control robots precisely in changing environments.

I completed my PhD in Electrical Engineering at Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU) as a Chinese Government Scholar in the IRMV Lab, Department of Automation. My thesis developed vision-based, finite-time control laws that let a quadrotor track and land on a platform that is both moving and tilting, using a single onboard camera. This work appeared in IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I and Robotics and Autonomous Systems.

I hold an MS in Electrical Engineering from Government College University, Lahore, and a BS in Electrical Engineering from UCP. I have taught since 2011, as a teaching assistant, lab engineer, lecturer and assistant professor, across both electrical engineering and computing. I've supervised more than 20 undergraduate projects, from vision-guided soccer robots to machine-learning malware detection, and received UCP's Best Project Supervisor Award in 2015 and 2016.

What I work on

Research

A robot that can see is only useful if it can act on what it sees, quickly and reliably. My work connects cameras to controllers.

Aerial Robotics & Autonomous Landing

Getting quadrotors to land on platforms that move and tilt, like a vehicle or a ship deck, using a single onboard camera. My controllers are guaranteed to converge in finite time.

Visual Servoing & Vision-based Control

Closing the loop between what a robot sees and how it moves. I design adaptive visual servo laws, including a virtual-reticle method, that stay robust when the target behaves unpredictably.

Mobile Robot Navigation

Vision-based path planning and obstacle avoidance for wheeled and omnidirectional robots. This work began with competition robots for RoboSprint and continues through student projects.

Robotics for Agriculture

Low-cost computer vision that helps field robots navigate crop rows on broad-acre farms, toward sustainable and affordable agricultural automation.

Vision-Based Autonomous Landing of Quadrotors on Moving & Tilting Platforms

Completed
  • Funding: Chinese Government Scholarship (doctoral research)
  • Host: IRMV Lab, Department of Automation, Shanghai Jiao Tong University
  • Role: Lead researcher (PhD)
  • Duration: 2017 – 2021

Landing a drone on a moving vehicle or a pitching ship deck is hard: the target moves, tilts, and may be partly out of view. I developed finite-time visual tracking and adaptive visual servo control laws that use a single onboard camera. The quadrotor converges on the platform within a guaranteed time instead of only approaching it gradually.

Outcomes
  • IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I (2023)
  • Robotics and Autonomous Systems (2021)
  • PhD thesis, SJTU (2025)

Real-Time Vision Module for a Humanoid Interactive Telerobot

Completed
  • Host: Government College University, Lahore
  • Role: Lead researcher (MS thesis)
  • Duration: 2011 – 2013

Designed a LabVIEW-based real-time image processing module for a humanoid interactive optomechatronic telerobot, giving the robot the visual feedback it needed to respond to people and objects around it.

Outcomes
  • MS thesis, GCU Lahore (2013)
  • Reusable LabVIEW vision pipeline later used in teaching labs

Vision-Based Navigation for Broad-Acre Agriculture Robots

Completed
  • Host: Faculty of Engineering, UCP
  • Role: Lead author, with K. M. Butt
  • Year: 2014

A computer-vision navigation module that lets field robots follow crop rows on large farms using inexpensive cameras, a step toward sustainable, affordable agricultural automation.

Outcomes

Multi-Agent Surveillance & Threat Evaluation

Completed
  • Host: Faculty of Engineering, UCP
